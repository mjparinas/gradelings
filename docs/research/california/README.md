## Files

- `fetch.sh` downloads the official files into `source/`, which is committed (downloaded 2026-10-14): the math, ELA and history–social science standards PDFs, the 2016 History–Social Science Framework, the 13 CA NGSS documents (Kindergarten to Grade 5 by grade, Grades 6 to 8 in the Preferred Integrated Model, high school by discipline), the middle school progressions document and chapters 5 and 7 of the 2016 Science Framework.
- `build_outcomes.py source` parses them into `outcomes.json`: `{"<grade>/<subject>": [{idx, kind, content, ...}]}` for Kindergarten (`k`) to Grade 9 and the subjects `math`, `language`, `science` and `social`. Run it with `python3 -I`. It needs `pypdf` and `pdfplumber` (both were already installed; pip was not needed). It takes about two minutes.
  - `ca: true` marks a math or ELA standard whose text (or one of its lettered parts) carries a California addition. CDE prints these in bold followed by "CA".
  - Science entries carry `ngss` (the same code, so this file has the same shape as Oregon's) and, for high school, `courses`: the courses of the Science Framework's High School Three-Course Model that teach the expectation (`Living Earth`, `Chemistry in the Earth System`, `Physics of the Universe`). It is read from chapter 7, where each course's instructional segments print their expectations in full.
  - Band standards are listed under every grade of the band: `K-2-ETS1` under Kindergarten to Grade 2 and `3-5-ETS1` under Grades 3 to 5 (CDE prints them in each grade's document), `MS-ETS1` under Grades 6 to 8, and every high school science expectation, the ELA 9–10 band, Algebra I and Principles of American Democracy under Grade 9.

### Parsing notes

- **Math:** read with pdfplumber from the standards pages (the ones with the grade tab, `K` to `8`, or `A1` for Algebra I). Footnotes and their markers are printed under 10 pt and dropped. Each entry is the main statement of a standard, not its lettered parts (`a.`, `b.`). Bracketed course notes such as "[Linear and exponential …]" are CDE's and are kept. The California additions are `2.NBT.2` (skip-count by 2s), `2.NBT.7.1` (estimation), `2.MD.7` (time relationships), `4.NF.7`, `4.G.2`, `5.OA.2.1` (prime factors), `A-CED.1` and `A-REI.3.1` (absolute value) and `F-LE.6` (quadratic models). Algebra I skips some numbers on purpose (`A-REI.2`, `A-REI.8`, `A-REI.9`, `F-LE.4`, `S-ID.4`), because those standards belong to other courses.
- **ELA:** the K–12 tables are landscape, three grades side by side. The parser reads each column between the table's ruled lines, so grades are never mixed. Some pages print the header row twice, a little apart and in two sizes; the parser reads one print layer at a time. Placeholders such as "(Not applicable to literature)" and "(Begins in grade 2)" are skipped. CDE prints `W.5.6` without its point ("6 With some guidance …"); a bare number counts only when it is the next number. Only the six K–12 strands are recorded. The Grades 6–12 literacy standards for history/social studies, science and technical subjects (`RH`, `RST`, `WHST`) are not in this record.
- **Science:** each entry is the performance expectation without its clarification statement and assessment boundary, and without the `*` engineering marker. pypdf's text layer splits a few words ("obser vations", "inf er"); the parser rejoins two pieces when pdfplumber's text of the same files has the joined word and one piece is not a word of its own, and prints the list.
- **History–social science:** read with pypdf. The Historical and Social Sciences Analysis Skills (K–5, 6–8 and 9–12) have no codes and are not recorded. The text layer splits one code ("1. 6 Students …" for `1.6`), which the parser allows only when the grade matches the standard before it. Soft hyphens at line ends are removed.
- **Text repairs:** two PDF line breaks are repaired by hand in `KNOWN_BREAKS` in `build_outcomes.py` ("distinguishes" in `RI.7.6`, "fresh water" in `5-ESS2-2`).

## Grade 9

- **Math:** California's high school standards come as model courses: the traditional pathway (Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II) and the integrated pathway (Mathematics I, II, III). Grade 9 uses **Algebra I**, as Washington and Oregon put their high school algebra under Grade 9. Algebra I has 50 standards. Mathematics I has 52; 39 are shared. Algebra I alone has rational exponents (`N-RN.1`–`3`), polynomial arithmetic and factoring (`A-APR.1`, `A-SSE.2`, `A-SSE.3`), quadratic equations (`A-REI.4`, `A-REI.7`), `F-IF.8`, `F-BF.4` and `F-LE.6`. Mathematics I alone has congruence and constructions (`G-CO.1`–`8`, `12`, `13`) and coordinate geometry (`G-GPE.4`, `5`, `7`). A district on the integrated pathway would need those Mathematics I codes added.
- **ELA:** the 9–10 band, as for Washington and Oregon.
- **Science:** all 71 high school expectations are under Grade 9, each with its Three-Course Model course or courses (37 entries name Living Earth, 21 Chemistry in the Earth System, 29 Physics of the Universe; a few appear in two or three). The Framework also has a Four-Course Model (chapter 8) and a three-year "Every Science, Every Year" model (appendix 4), and says districts may arrange expectations differently as long as all students meet all of them. It does not fix the order of the courses. Keep it simple: treat the high school set as one Grade 9 pool, as Oregon does, and prefer Living Earth (biology with Earth science) when a unit needs one course.
- **Social studies:** California has no required Grade 9 course. The 2016 Framework (chapter 14, "Grade Nine: Elective Courses in History–Social Science") describes two semesters of electives: World and Regional Geography, Modern California, Physical Geography, Survey of World Religions, The Humanities, Anthropology, Psychology, Sociology, Women in United States History, Ethnic Studies, Law-Related Education and Financial Literacy. None has its own content standards.
  - **Ethnic studies:** AB 101 (2021) adds a one-semester ethnic studies course to the graduation requirements (Education Code 51225.3), starting with students graduating in 2029–30 (the class of 2030), with high schools required to offer it from 2025–26. The duty applies only when the Legislature funds it. News reports say the 2025–26 state budget did not, and that districts treat the requirement as not yet in effect. CDE's ethnic studies pages could not be read (see "Access" below), so check the status there before writing about it. The Ethnic Studies Model Curriculum (adopted March 2021) is guidance, not standards.
  - **Recommendation for `9/social`:** follow Washington's precedent, which the owner approved ("Grade 9 social studies is civics"). The closest California civics standards are the Grade 12 **Principles of American Democracy** standards (`12.1` to `12.10`: the Constitution's principles, rights and obligations, civil society, the three branches, the courts, elections, state and local government, the media, comparative government and tensions in a constitutional democracy). This record puts them under Grade 9. They are written for Grade 12, not Grade 9, and the README and any parent page should say so plainly: "California has no Grade 9 social studies course; these units practise the state's civics standards, taught in Grade 12."
  - The Grade 12 **Principles of Economics** standards reuse the numbers `12.1` to `12.6`, so they are left out to keep each code unique. If California wants economics units in Grade 9 (as Oregon has), cite them with a prefix, for example `Econ 12.2.1`, and add them to the parser.

The Framework's table of contents gives chapter 12 (Grade 8) the subtitle "Continuity and Change in Modern United States History", which is Grade 11's. The chapter's own title page says "Growth and Conflict", as the standards do.

**California Indian content.** Grade 3 (`3.2`) and Grade 4 (`4.2.1`, the major nations of California Indians) ask for California Indian content, and `4.2.1` mentions "legends, and religious beliefs". Our rules apply: keep it light and in the present tense, never treat all Nations as one, no sacred or ceremonial details (so units skip the beliefs part of `4.2.1`), and borrow no other state's Tribal content (Washington's and Oregon's Tribal units stay out). AB 738 (2017, Education Code 51226.9) directs a Native American studies model curriculum for high school; its adoption could not be confirmed on CDE's site. California units about California Indian nations are written for California and name each Nation as it names itself.

**Ethnic studies is sensitive** in California (the model curriculum's drafts and AB 101's later guardrail bills were widely debated). Keep any units on identity, immigration or civil rights factual and neutral, tied to the content standards, and in the same tone as the rest of the app.

## Report card and assessments

- **Report card:** CDE sets no statewide student report-card scale. Districts design their own report cards. Many elementary schools use standards-based marks from 1 to 4; middle and high schools give letter grades. Like Washington and Oregon, the framework should use four practice steps (Beginning, Approaching, Meeting, Exceeding), with the "practice, not a report-card mark" rule.
- **State tests:** the California Assessment of Student Performance and Progress (CAASPP).
  - Smarter Balanced English language arts/literacy and mathematics in Grades 3 to 8 and 11.
  - The California Science Test (CAST) in Grades 5 and 8 and once in high school (Grade 10, 11 or 12; CAST blueprint, approved by the State Board 8 November 2017).
  - California Alternate Assessments for students with significant cognitive disabilities; the California Spanish Assessment and the Grade Two Diagnostic Assessments are optional. English learners also take the ELPAC, which is not part of CAASPP.
  - **Parent/guardian exemption:** under Education Code 60615 a parent or guardian may submit a written request each year to excuse their child from any or all parts of CAASPP. Districts must tell parents about this right each year (CDE's notification letter page, reviewed 22 September 2026). A guide should state the right plainly and neutrally and point to the school.
  - Dates and test details change each year, so guides should point to CDE (cde.ca.gov/ta/tg/ca/).
- **Social-emotional learning:** CDE publishes the California SEL Guiding Principles (full and summary versions), an SEL resource guide and the Transformative SEL (T-SEL) Competencies and Conditions for Thriving, "developed for voluntary use". The five competencies are Self-Awareness, Self-Management, Social Awareness, Relationship Skills and Responsible Decision-Making. They are guidance, not graded standards, so they are the candidate for the competencies page (`/guides/california/sel-standards/` or similar, matching Washington's and Oregon's SEL guides).

## Access

- cde.ca.gov sits behind a Radware bot filter. On 13 October 2026 every PDF in `fetch.sh` downloaded with a browser user agent. HTML pages answered with a "Radware Captcha Page" (HTTP 200) after the first few requests, so the CAASPP, ethnic studies and SEL pages were read through a fetch tool where it worked, and through CDE PDFs (the CAST blueprint). `fetch.sh` checks that every file is a real PDF and pauses between downloads; if it stops with "not a PDF", wait a few minutes and run it again.
- The ethnic studies funding status and the Native American studies model curriculum could not be confirmed on CDE's own pages. No codes in this record come from anywhere but the CDE PDFs.

## Known gaps

- **Coverage (October 2026):** every math, science and history–social science standard from Kindergarten to Grade 9 is cited by at least one unit, except high school science outside Living Earth (below), and every Reading, Reading Foundational Skills and Language standard in ELA. California-only units (`ca-` ids, `src/content/california/units/`) fill what no shared or adopted unit practises. `california.test.ts` ("has a unit for every standard a child can practise in the app") fails when a practisable standard has no unit.
- **Not practised, on purpose:** Writing (`W`), Speaking and Listening (`SL`), reading fluency (`RF.x.4`) and "read a range of texts" (`RL.x.10`, `RI.x.10`) need the child to write, speak or record, and the app takes no free text or voice from children. A few exceptions are tap questions: `SL.x.2` on read-aloud passages, `W.1.8` (answering a question from given sources). Grade 9 science cites the Living Earth expectations; those taught only in Chemistry in the Earth System or Physics of the Universe have no unit. A few expectations about building a model or simulation (`MS-ETS1-4`, `HS-ETS1-4`, `HS-LS4-6`, `HS-ESS3-6`) are practised by reading and judging a model's results, not making one.
- **Math:** Grade 9 records Algebra I only (see above).
- **ELA:** literacy in history/social studies, science and technical subjects (`RH`, `RST`, `WHST`, Grades 6–12) is not recorded. Washington's Research and Media Literacy units would cite `W.7`–`W.9`, `SL.2` and `RI.7`–`RI.9` instead, as in Oregon.
- **Science:** middle school follows the Preferred Integrated Model. The Discipline-Specific Model (Earth science in Grade 6, life science in Grade 7, physical science in Grade 8) is the State Board's alternative, and districts choose. The grade in this record is CDE's preferred one, not a rule. California's Environmental Principles and Concepts are not recorded.
- **Social studies:** the analysis skills have no codes. Principles of Economics is left out (duplicate numbers). Grade 9 cites Grade 12 standards.
- **ELA text:** see "Known untidy text" above.

## What changes from Washington and Oregon

- **Math codes:** California keeps the original Common Core codes (`4.NBT.1`, no cluster letter), with California additions (`2.NBT.7.1`). Washington uses OSPI's 2026 codes (`M.4.Q.NBT.2`) and Oregon its own domains (`4.GM`, `6.AEE`). The content is plain Common Core plus a few additions, so it is closer to Washington's than to Oregon's: Grade 3 has inch line plots (`3.MD.4`), Grade 8 solves systems algebraically (`8.EE.8`), and Algebra I has polynomial arithmetic, factoring and quadratics, which Oregon's high school standards lack.
- **ELA:** California keeps `RL` and `RI` apart, like Oregon; Washington merges them (`R`) and adds `RML`. California's codes are strand first (`RL.4.1`); Oregon's have an `ELA.` prefix and Washington's are grade first.
- **Science codes:** California cites NGSS codes unchanged (`4-PS3-2`), as Washington does; Oregon has its own dotted codes. But middle school is placed by grade (the Preferred Integrated Model), as in Oregon, and the placement differs from both: for example `MS-PS1` (matter and reactions) is Grade 7, `MS-PS2` and `MS-PS4` are Grade 8, `MS-PS3-3`–`5` and `MS-ESS2-4`–`6` (weather, climate) are Grade 6, `MS-LS3-2` is Grade 6 and `MS-LS3-1` Grade 8.
- **Social studies:** California's codes, themes and grades differ from both. Grade 4 is California, Grade 6 ancient civilizations and Grade 7 medieval and early modern times (like Oregon's 6/7 band split), and Grade 8 runs to 1914 (Oregon stops at 1865 and Washington includes Reconstruction). Grade 9 has no course; Washington's and Oregon's Grade 9 civics is the model.
- **Assessments:** CAASPP (Smarter Balanced in Grades 3–8 and 11) is like Oregon's OSAS in timing; Washington tests high school in Grade 10. Science (CAST) is Grades 5, 8 and once in high school. California has a written parent exemption right (Education Code 60615); Oregon's opt-out is ORS 329.479.
- **SEL:** California's T-SEL competencies are voluntary guidance, like Oregon's Transformative SEL and Washington's SEL standards.

- **ELA:** all of it carries over.
- **Science:** all shared units carry over, with California's own middle school grades (`mixtures` and `particles-heat-ab` to Grade 7, `work-energy-8` to Grade 6). About 12 Washington-only units name Washington places and need `ca-` copies, as Oregon needed `or-` copies.
- **Social studies:** Grades K–2, 5, 6, 8 and 9 carry over in large part; Grade 7 comes mostly from Oregon. Grade 3 (the local region) and Grade 4 (California) need California-written units, as do the California Indian units.
