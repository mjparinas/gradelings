Pennsylvania social studies, Grades 5 to 9: codes no unit practises, and why
============================================================================

PDE's social studies standards repeat every area at every grade and assign no topics to grades, so the grade sequence
(5 early US history, 6 world cultures and economics, 7 US history and government, 8 Civil War to the twentieth
century with civics, 9 civics and government) is the owner's. Every code a unit cites was checked against
outcomes.json (contract test). Uncovered, in all five grades:

- 8.1.x.B and 8.1.x.C (compare points of view in primary and secondary sources; produce an organized product with a
  thesis): need free writing, research and sources. One 8.1.x.B-style unit exists at Grade 6 (mb-evidence-8) and Grade 5
  (or-g5-history-detective); the writing and thesis codes (8.1.x.C) have no tap-to-answer form.
- Speaking, writing and presentation parts of any standard (for example 5.2.x.D "participate in the political process" as an
  activity, 5.1.x.F "use of symbols" beyond multiple choice).
- 8.4.x (world history compare and contrast) in Grades 5, 7 and 8: world history is taught in Grade 6 (and in part in
  Grade 8 through the two world wars); 8.4.x.A and 8.4.x.B are cited only where a unit practises them.
- 7.x geography in Grades 5, 6 and 8: Grade 5 has states and capitals (7.1.5.B) and Grade 6 world maps and regions; Grade 7 holds
  Pennsylvania's own geography unit (pa-g7-pa-geography). 7.2, 7.3 and 7.4 at Grades 5, 6 and 8 are only partly practised
  (maps-graphs-8 covers 7.1.8.A and 7.3.8.A).
- 5.4.x foreign policy in Grades 5 to 7: practised in Grade 8 (pa-g8-world-media) and Grade 9 (pa-g9-foreign-policy and the
  adopted Cold War and world today units); 5.4.x.C (organizations solving common problems) and 5.4.x.E (interest
  groups' foreign policy politics) only appear in those two grades.
- 6.x economics codes beyond those the adopted and pa- units practise: 6.1.x.F and H (marginal analysis), 6.3.9-12.E to I at
  depth (tax structures, externalities, public goods), 6.4.9-12.C, E, F and G (monetary policy detail, exchange of
  nongovernmental institutions); Grades 5 to 8 only cover the banded 3-5 and 6-8 codes cited in the plan.
- 17.x personal finance: 17.1.x (goals, mindset, behavioral biases, financial technology, wills and powers of attorney),
  17.2.9-12.A to G (income types, post-secondary cost decisions, benefits, self-employment), 17.3.9-12.G to M (payment methods,
  housing, fundraising), 17.4.9-12.D, F, G, H and J (rates of return, advice, benchmark indices, portfolios, risk
  attitudes) and 17.5.9-12.A, B, D to G (risk, insurance comparison, public insurance) have no unit. Grades 5 to 8 practise
  budgeting, saving, interest and credit only (az-g8-personal-finance, or-g5-earn-and-save, pa-g6-money-basics).
- Grade 9: of 276 codes, only the 9 column and the banded 9-12 codes are cited. The C, U and W columns (the course-specific
  Civics and Government, US History and World History forms) are not cited, because they repeat the 9 column's ideas for
  courses this app does not teach separately. 5.1.9.F (symbols in civil disobedience) is practised only by
  pa-g9-foreign-policy; 5.3.9.C and 5.3.9.G are partly practised.
- Grades 5, 7 and 8: 8.2.x.A, B and D (contributions, documents and places, conflict and cooperation in Pennsylvania) are
  practised by pa-g5-penns-colony, pa-g5-lenape-and-neighbors, pa-g5-philadelphia-nation, pa-g7-pa-new-nation,
  pa-g7-pennsylvanians, pa-g8-gettysburg, pa-g8-freedom-seekers and pa-g8-industry-immigration; Pennsylvania
  history after the 1970s, and the Grade 7 Pennsylvania history from 1840 on, have no unit.
- Native peoples: content is light, in the present tense and from FACTS.md only. No federally recognized tribe resides in
  Pennsylvania, so units say Lenape, Susquehannock, Shawnee and other nations' descendants live elsewhere. It needs
  partner review before it grows. Nothing sacred or ceremonial is included.
- Facts not used because they are UNCONFIRMED in FACTS.md: the year William Penn arrived, an exact count of tribes with ancestral
  ties, the meaning of the "clay pillow" on the coat of arms, and the Erie as a nation.
