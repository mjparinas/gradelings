# Pennsylvania (`us-pa`): facts for parent guides, privacy text and content writers

Every bullet is a fact read on **2026-10-23** from the URL given. Anything not confirmed on an official page is marked **UNCONFIRMED**. "Code" means the Pennsylvania Code as published at pacodeandbulletin.gov, which on that day reflected changes through 56 Pa.B. 5370 (August 8, 2026).

Where the research brief's premise was slightly off, the bullet says **CORRECTION**.

## 1. State tests

### PSSA (grades 3 to 8)
- ELA and math are given in grades 3 to 8; science in **grades 5 and 8**. **CORRECTION:** the old rule was science in grades 4 and 8; the regulation says that beginning with 2025-26 the PSSA science test is given at grade 5 and grade 8 and the grade 4 science test is discontinued. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.51a.html (22 Pa. Code 4.51a(c)); PDE parent FAQ lists "Science, grades 5 and 8": https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams/administration-materials/pssa%20parent%20faq.pdf
- Spring 2026 windows (the year just finished): ELA April 20 to 24, 2026; math and science April 27 to May 8, 2026 (includes make-ups). Same parent FAQ as above.
- Spring 2027 windows (current school year): ELA April 26 to 30, 2027; math, science and make-ups May 3 to 7, 2027; optional math, science and make-ups May 10 to 14, 2027. Spring 2028: ELA April 24 to 28, 2028; math/science May 1 to 5, with an optional week May 8 to 12. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/pennsylvania-system-of-school-assessment-pssa (section "Testing Windows"). Usual pattern: ELA in the last full week of April, math and science the following one or two weeks, all online. Schedules change every year, so a guide should say "usually spring (April to early May)" and point to PDE's PSSA page.
- All PSSA testing is online. The 2026 Keystone page says districts use the online format; the PSSA DAC training pages are titled "online administration spring 2026". https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/pennsylvania-system-of-school-assessment-pssa
- Writing: ELA includes a written response. Grade 3 has a short-answer question; grades 4 to 8 have a **Text Dependent Analysis (TDA)**, an essay about a passage, scored on content and writing skills. Language and writing standards are also tested through multiple-choice Conventions of Standard English items. PSSA FAQ above; https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/pennsylvania-system-of-school-assessment-pssa
- Lengths (PDE parent FAQ): ELA about 3 to 4 hours, math about 2 to 3 hours, science about 1 to 2 hours, across several sessions. Question types: multiple choice, technology-enhanced, constructed response / open-ended. Same FAQ.
- Achievement levels are **Advanced, Proficient, Basic and Below Basic** (22 Pa. Code 4.51a(a)(4)). Cut scores are approved by the State Board. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.51a.html
- What PSSA science measures: four reporting categories, Technology and Engineering; Life Sciences and Environmental Literacy and Sustainability; Physical Sciences; Earth and Space Sciences (PSSA FAQ). The regulation says PSSA science is based on the standards for "Science, Environment, Ecology, Technology and Engineering" (Appendix B-1) from July 1, 2025 (22 Pa. Code 4.51a(b)). PDE's own assessment pages still use the name **STEELS** ("Science is aligned to STEELS and the dimensions in the Foundation Boxes", https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/classroom-diagnostic-tools-cdt). The final standards documents themselves are titled "Pennsylvania Integrated Standards for Science, Environment, Ecology, Technology and Engineering (Grades K-5)" and "...Science, Environment and Ecology (Grades 6-12)" and never use the word STEELS (read in the PDFs, see section 5).
- Individual results go to schools and parents; the state may collect only aggregate scores by school and district (22 Pa. Code 4.51(g)). State assessments must include student names so parents and teachers get results (4.51(e)). https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.51.html
- 22 Pa. Code 4.21(k): students who have not reached proficiency in reading and math by the end of grade 5 on the PSSA must be given instructional opportunities to reach it. 4.21(j): students who have not reached proficiency in reading and math in K to 3, as the school decides, get a grade-level learning plan. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.21.html

### Keystone Exams (high school)
- End-of-course exams in **Algebra I, Literature and Biology**, built to replace the grade 11 PSSA. Taken at or near the end of the course; results are "banked" until junior year for accountability and senior year for graduation. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams ; parent FAQ https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/keystone-exams/administration-materials/english%20parent%20faq%202026%20keystone.pdf
- Offered three times a year (the regulation says fall, spring and summer; PDE now runs winter, spring and summer). 2026-27 windows: Winter Wave 1 Dec 7 to 18, 2026; Winter Wave 2 Jan 4 to 15, 2027; Spring May 17 to 28, 2027; Summer July 26 to 30, 2027. Keystone page above; 22 Pa. Code 4.51b(b).
- Achievement levels are the same four. Scaled-score cut points (page above): Algebra I Proficient 1500 to 1545, Biology 1500 to 1548, Literature 1500 to 1583; Basic starts at 1439, 1460 and 1444 respectively.
- Graduation status: **Act 158 of 2018** (Oct. 24, 2018, SB 1095) amended School Code section 121. Keystone proficiency is not required to graduate. A student is deemed proficient if they (1) reach a satisfactory composite score on the three Keystones (at least Proficient on one and at least Basic on the other two), or (2) complete locally set grade-based requirements plus one of: an approved alternative assessment score (SAT, PSAT, ACT, Armed Forces test), ACT WorkKeys Gold, a postsecondary-readiness evidence route, or a CTE route. https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?txtType=PDF&SessYr=2018&ActNum=0158.&SessInd=0 (fetched with a browser user agent; plain curl gets 403)
- PDE: "Effective with the graduating class of 2023, Pennsylvania high school students have the option to demonstrate their postsecondary preparedness and meet statewide requirements through one of **five Pathways to Graduation**." The Keystone Exams must still be taken for federal accountability. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/graduation-requirements . The five pathway names are not on that page: **UNCONFIRMED** (district pages say Keystone Proficiency, Keystone Composite, Alternate Assessment, Evidence-Based, Career and Technical Education; none read from a PDE or legislative page).
- 22 Pa. Code 4.24(c) (beginning 2022-23): graduation requires course completion and grades, local proficiency in standards not state-tested, and proficiency in ELA, math and science through Keystone proficiency, a School Code section 121 pathway, or a locally validated assessment. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.24.html ; last source note on that page is July 15, 2022.
- Changes 2024 to 2026: I found **none**. The Code page read today shows no amendment to 4.24 after 2022, and PDE's graduation page is unchanged in substance. UNCONFIRMED only in the sense that I cannot prove a negative; check PDE before a guide says "no change".
- Which grade a student takes each Keystone in is not fixed: PDE says only "at or near the end of a Keystone-related course" (and accountability testing is by spring of grade 11). A guide for grade 9 should not name a grade for the Keystones; a specific grade is **UNCONFIRMED**.

### Alternate assessment and classroom tools
- **PASA (Pennsylvania Alternate System of Assessment, DLM)**: adaptive alternate assessment in ELA, math and science for students with the most significant cognitive disabilities; the IEP team decides, and must answer "yes" to all six eligibility criteria. 2026-27 test window March 8 to May 7, 2027. Grades tested: **UNCONFIRMED** on that page. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/pennsylvania-alternate-system-of-assessment-pasa
- **Classroom Diagnostic Tools (CDT)**: voluntary, computer-adaptive diagnostic tests offered in grades 3 to high school (reading, writing, math, science, high-school courses); results guide instruction, and PDE says it is not a predictor of the PSSA. 2026-27: literacy and math testing window Aug 24, 2026 to June 11, 2027; science from Oct 14, 2026. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/classroom-diagnostic-tools-cdt
- **Firefly Student Progress Indicators** (new, not in the brief): online benchmark assessments in ELA and math (grades 3 to 8), science (grades 5 and 8), Algebra I, Biology and Literature; "completes Pennsylvania's innovative assessment system" next to the CDT and the PSSA/Keystone; predictions of end-of-year results begin 2025-26 for ELA and math, 2026-27 for science. 2026-27 window Oct 5, 2026 to Apr 16, 2027 (fall, winter, spring seasons). https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/assessment-and-accountability/firefly

### Can a parent opt a child out?
- Only by the religious-belief route. 22 Pa. Code **4.4(d)(4)** (not (d)(3), which is about excusal from specific *instruction*): school entities must give parents the right to review a state assessment in the school at convenient hours at least 2 weeks before it is given, to decide whether it conflicts with their religious belief; if it does and the parent wants the child excused, "the right of the parents or guardians will not be denied upon written request that states the objection" to the superintendent, charter CEO or AVTS director. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.4.html
- PDE parent FAQ: the religious belief "is the only reason allowable under regulation"; reviewers sign a confidentiality agreement and no copies or notes may leave the school. PSSA parent FAQ above.
- 22 Pa. Code 4.4(d)(3): parents can have a child excused from specific instruction that conflicts with their religious beliefs on written request; (d)(5): the right to have the child excluded from research studies or surveys by outside entities unless prior written consent was given. Same page.
- Whether PDE treats a parent's plain refusal as a recognised exclusion (it reportedly does not, and it counts against the school's participation rate): **UNCONFIRMED**, seen only in search-result summaries of the PSSA handbook, not read directly.

## 2. Report cards, accountability, reading law

- **No statewide student report-card scale.** I searched the full text of 22 Pa. Code Chapter 4 (the standards and assessment chapter) for "report card", "grading", "grade point" and "letter grade": no match. The standards say what students should learn; each school entity designs curriculum and assessment (4.4(a)-(b): "the local curriculum be designed by school entities"; "local school entities have the greatest possible flexibility in curriculum planning"). https://www.pa.gov/agencies/stateboard/resources/regulations--policy/state-academic-standards (Chapter 4 link) and the Chapter 4 PDF https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/022_0004.pdf . Chapter 12 (student rights) section 12.31 only requires each board to adopt a plan for student records (https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter12/s12.31.html). No PDE page was found that sets a grading scale: **UNCONFIRMED** as an absolute negative (I did not search the whole School Code or every PDE guidance document), but the copy "Pennsylvania doesn't have one statewide report card; each school district or charter decides" is supported.
- 180-day school year and minimum hours: 22 Pa. Code 11.1 (180 days; 900 elementary and 450 kindergarten hours, 990 secondary). https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter11/022_0011.pdf (a 2023 copy; the Code page shows section 11.13 amended in 2022).
- **Future Ready PA Index**: PDE's public school progress tool, launched November 2018, a dashboard with no single summative score, showing three colour-coded categories: academic performance, student progress, and college and career readiness. It is not a student report card. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/schools/every-student-succeeds-act-essa/future-ready-pa-index
- **PA School Performance Profile (SPP)**: the older (pre-2018) school score; its site now sends visitors to the Future Ready PA Index and says its scores cover the 2016-17 school year. Not a student report card. https://paschoolperformance.org/
- **ESSA State Report Card**: PDE's school, district and student-group data report reached through the Future Ready PA Index. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/schools/every-student-succeeds-act-essa/essa-report-card
- **Third-grade retention law: none found.** The state's early-literacy law (below) requires screening, parent notice and an intervention plan with the goal "that the student can read at or above grade level by the end of grade three"; it does not mention holding a student back. I searched the full text of Act 47 for "retain/retention/promot": nothing relevant. 22 Pa. Code 4.21(j) and (k) require learning plans and extra instruction, not retention. UNCONFIRMED only as a universal negative.
- **Early literacy / science of reading**: it is **Act 55 of 2022** (Chapter 49 of the Code and section 1205.8 of the School Code), not 2023: structured-literacy training for educators, with PDE's structured-literacy competencies for teacher preparation. Then **Act 135 of 2024** and **Act 47 of 2025** (SB 315, approved Nov. 12, 2025; Article XV-N of the School Code). https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/curriculum/english-language-arts/act-135 ; statute text https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?txtType=PDF&SessYr=2025&ActNum=0047.&SessInd=0
- What Act 47 requires of schools (sections 1501-N to 1509-N, read in the statute): by the beginning of **2027-28**, adopt evidence-based reading curriculum for K to 3 (they need not pick from PDE's list; PDE FAQ https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/schools/school-services/act-47-structured-literacy-faqs ); screen every K to grade 3 student for reading **three times a year** (beginning, middle, end) with an approved universal screener; students identified "at risk for reading failure" are identified as having a reading deficiency; parents of kindergartners who show a deficiency on the third screening, and of grade 1 to 3 students identified during the year, are notified in writing or electronically, with progress updates three times a year; each such student gets a reading intervention plan. Screening data reporting to PDE begins July 31, 2028 (statute, 1509-N); curriculum and training reporting July 31, 2027 (PDE FAQ). The approved screener list was still "forthcoming" on PDE's FAQ page.

## 3. Student data privacy: what binds a vendor like us

- **No Pennsylvania student online privacy statute binding technology providers has been enacted** (nothing like Oregon's OSIPA or Florida's section 1006.1494). Evidence:
  - **SB 378 (2025-26), "Student Data Privacy and Protection Act"**, would add Chapter 5 to Title 24: third parties under contract with a school entity could use student data only to provide the contracted service and could not do targeted marketing, build profiles (beyond the contract or the school's purposes), sell or exchange student data, or block a parent's export. It was reported out of the Senate Education Committee March 23, 2026; **last action "Laid on the table," June 3, 2026**. Not passed the Senate, not in the House, not law. Status page https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/sb378 ; text https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/text/PDF/2025/0/SB0378/PN1528
  - **HB 78 (2025-26), consumer data privacy act**: passed the House 127 to 76 on Oct. 1, 2025; in the Senate, last action "Removed from table," Oct. 6, 2026. Not law. https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb78 . If it becomes law it would bind us as a controller (it includes under-13 consent rules); recheck before launch.
  - I did not find an enacted act through other searches. **UNCONFIRMED** only as a universal negative (the search tool also surfaced a district page saying Pennsylvania is one of the few states without a student data privacy law, which is a district's statement, not an official source).
- **What does bind us in Pennsylvania:**
  - **Breach of Personal Information Notification Act** (Act of Dec. 22, 2005, P.L. 474, No. 94; 73 P.S. 2301 et seq.). "Personal information" is first name or initial plus last name with a Social Security, driver's licence or state ID, financial account, medical (state-agency), health insurance number, **or a user name or e-mail address with a password or security question that lets someone into an online account**. An entity that keeps such data must notify affected Pennsylvania residents "without unreasonable delay" after determining a breach. https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/?p=8394
  - **Act 33 of 2024** (SB 824, approved by the Governor **June 28, 2024**, in force **September 26, 2024**, P.L. 427): added notice to the **Attorney General at the same time as individuals when more than 500 Pennsylvania residents are affected**, notice to consumer reporting agencies above 500 people (down from 1,000), and 12 months of free credit monitoring and reports where Social Security, driver's licence, state ID or bank account numbers were involved. https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?txtType=PDF&SessYr=2024&ActNum=0033.&SessInd=0 ; https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2023/sb824 . **CORRECTION:** the Attorney General's page says "July 28, 2024"; the General Assembly's bill history says June 28, 2024, and the 90-day effective date of Sept. 26 only works from June 28.
  - **Act 151 of 2022** (SB 696, Nov. 3, 2022, in force 180 days later, May 2023): "a county, **public school** or municipality" must notify affected people within **seven business days** of determining a breach and the county district attorney within **three business days**; "public school" means a school district, intermediate unit, charter school, cyber charter school or area career and technical school. **A private company is not a "public school", so these deadlines do not bind us.** Section 3(c): **a vendor that maintains, stores or manages computerized data on behalf of another entity must notify that entity "following discovery"**; the entity then does the rest. The statute gives the vendor no number of days. https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?txtType=PDF&SessYr=2022&ActNum=0151.&SessInd=0
  - **Practical reading for `/privacy/` and `docs/school/`**: a Pennsylvania school (or a family) can hold us to (a) telling the school without delay if we discover a breach of data we hold for it, and (b) the general breach-notice duty for residents' personal information. A "7 business days" or "within X days" promise to schools is a contract term we may offer, not a statutory one. Our data (first name, grade, practice results, no last names, birth year optional) mostly falls outside "personal information" as the Act defines it unless an account email and password are involved.
- **Other Pennsylvania rules that touch student data but bind schools, not vendors:**
  - 22 Pa. Code 4.4(d)(5): parents may exclude a child from research studies or surveys by outside entities unless they gave prior written consent. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.4.html
  - 22 Pa. Code 4.12(j): PDE may not expand student data collection or collect personal family data because of the PA Core Standards. https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.12.html
  - 22 Pa. Code 12.31 and 12.32: each school board adopts a student-records plan that must conform to state and federal law. Same site, chapter 12.
- **The Public School Code does not require districts to sign data agreements with vendors**: no such section found. In practice many Pennsylvania districts use the Student Data Privacy Consortium's shared agreement through the "Pennsylvania Student Privacy Alliance" (district page https://www.hempfieldsd.org/operations/technology-department/data-privacy ; IU13 page https://www.iu13.org/administrators/statewide-software-sales/software-for-schools/sdpc/ ; the consortium's own page returned a login error when read). This is a practice, not a law. **UNCONFIRMED** whether a PDE page says anything about it; none found.
- **FERPA, PPRA and COPPA** are federal and apply as everywhere; Pennsylvania adds nothing beyond the items above.

## 4. A statewide "competencies" framework for a parent guide

### Candidates read
1. **Pennsylvania Career Ready Skills (PA CRS)**: PDE's own social and emotional learning framework. Official text: "The Pennsylvania Career Ready Skills (PA CRS) are **social emotional learning progressions** that support the development of student competence ... grouped into three domains." Guidance, **not regulation**: the toolkit says "the integration of these skills into classrooms and schools is optional". Toolkit "Final July 2018". It runs from PreK to grade 12 in grade bands PK-K, 1-5, 6-8 and 9-12. The new science standards (effective July 1, 2025) list "Pennsylvania career ready skills continuum" among their sources. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/student/career-ready-pa/pennsylvania-career-ready-skills ; toolkit https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/student/career-ready-pa/pennsylvania-career-ready-skills/pa-career-ready-skills-toolkit
2. **Career Education and Work standards (Appendix E-1, 22 Pa. Code Chapter 4, area 13)**: regulation, **effective July 1, 2026** (published in the Pennsylvania Bulletin Feb. 8, 2025), grade-banded K-2, 3-5, 6-8, 9-12; four areas 13.1 Career Awareness and Exploration, 13.2 Employability Skills, 13.3 Growth and Advancement, 13.4 Personal Interests and Career Planning. Binding on schools, but career-planning in tone. https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/stateboard/documents/regulations-and-statements/state-academic-standards/career%20education%20and%20work%20standards%20(appendix%20e-1).pdf
3. **PA Core Standards for Mathematical Practice**: the eight practices, part of the PA Core Mathematics standards (Appendix A-2, effective March 1, 2014), binding and K-12. Same eight as the Common Core; Arizona's guide in this repo already uses this approach. Math only.
4. **PDE has no separate state SEL standard.** The only SEL material PDE publishes is the PA CRS (plus early learning standards for pre-K and a 2020 "Staff and Student Wellness Guide"). **UNCONFIRMED** as a universal negative; I found no SEL standard in Chapter 4.

### Recommendation: **Pennsylvania Career Ready Skills** (the single best fit)
It is the Commonwealth's own SEL competency framework, it covers every grade this app teaches with kid-readable "I CAN" statements, and it parallels the SEL guides for Washington, Oregon, California, New York and Minnesota. Say plainly it is PDE guidance, optional for schools, and not graded. If the owner wants a binding source, use the Career Education and Work employability skills (13.2) as a "see also"; do not mix the two lists.

**Official wording** (toolkit chart "A Career Ready Graduate" and the PA CRS page):
- **Self-Awareness and Self-Management** ("Recognize and regulate emotions"): "skills to understand and manage behavior, including the abilities to set goals, recognize feelings, and respond effectively to challenges."
- **Establishing and Maintaining Relationships** ("Communicate and collaborate amongst diversity"): "skills that support healthy relationships, including awareness and respect of diversity, and the abilities to communicate and resolve conflicts effectively."
- **Social Problem-Solving Skills** ("Demonstrate empathy and respectful choice"): "skills that support students to understand social norms, make responsible choices, and engage effectively in diverse contexts."

The toolkit says each skill should be acquired by the end of the grade band. Sample band statements (continuum, "The learner will..." with the child's "I CAN" form):
- Self-Awareness, PK-K: "Recognize and label basic feelings"; "Express a want and the means to achieve it" (I can say what I want). Grades 1 to 5: "Identify different ways of expressing a feeling"; "Select coping skill strategies in response to adverse situations" (I can stick to a challenging task); "Distinguish among and set short term, mid-range, and long-term goals." Grades 6 to 8: "Identify behavioral expressions of feelings within a context"; "Identify and evaluate distractors that impact reaching ones' goals." Grades 9 to 12: "Evaluate behaviors in relation to the impact on self and others"; "Advocate for oneself in education, employment, and within the community."
- Relationships, PK-K: "Interact in pro-social ways (e.g., reciprocal conversation, turn taking, sharing) with peers and adults"; "Identify similarities and differences between self and others." Grades 1 to 5: "Recognize that conflict occurs and identify ways to respond"; "Explain ways to establish relationships that are positive and supportive of others"; "Demonstrate respect for the uniqueness of others." Grades 6 to 8: "Explain how empathy and perspective taking foster relationship building."
- Social Problem-Solving, PK-K: "Acknowledge the consequences of choices"; "Identify similarities and differences of various social contexts"; "Recognize and respond to the feelings of others" (I can help others in need). Grades 1 to 5 and 6 to 8: the same three ideas at greater depth, including "Identify consequences of a decision to oneself and others prior to action."
(Full continuum PDF is linked from the toolkit page; the page text above was read from the toolkit page on 2026-10-23.)

**Secondary list, if a guide wants a second tier** (Career Education and Work 13.2 Employability Skills, one skill each; official headline at grades K to 2 and 3 to 5):
- Critical thinking and problem solving: "Practice solving problems individually and in groups" (K-2); "Develop the traits needed to solve problems" (3-5).
- Oral and written communication: "Use context clues to identify how communication varies within different social situations in varied environments."
- Teamwork and collaboration: "Demonstrate cooperation and positive interactions with classmates, recognizing that people have different backgrounds, experiences, beliefs, and ideas."
- Technology: "Demonstrate proper and safe Internet and instructional technology use."
- Time and project management: "Discuss how time is structured at home and school" (K-2); "Develop a personal schedule based on activities and responsibilities at both home and school" (3-5).
- Entrepreneurial mindset (grades 3 to 5 up): "List and describe several characteristics of the Entrepreneurial Mindset characteristics."
Also 13.3 Growth and Advancement: "Set and achieve a goal" (K-2); "Set and achieve S.M.A.R.T. goals" (3-5).

**The eight Standards for Mathematical Practice** (names as PDE hosts them, https://static.pdesas.org/content/documents/Bulleted_Mathematical_Practices.pdf ; PDE's math page restates five habits: https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/programs-and-services/instruction/elementary-and-secondary-education/curriculum/mathematics): 1 Make sense of problems and persevere in solving them; 2 Reason abstractly and quantitatively; 3 Construct viable arguments and critique the reasoning of others; 4 Model with mathematics; 5 Use appropriate tools strategically; 6 Attend to precision; 7 Look for and make use of structure; 8 Look for and express regularity in repeated reasoning. The official Pennsylvania Core Mathematics PDF on pa.gov is a scan with garbled text, so the quoted wording is from the pdesas.org copy. Note PDE's current math page wording ("Make sense of and persevere in solving complex and novel mathematical problems") differs from the older practice names.

## 5. Grade structure and standards status

### Compulsory age and kindergarten
- Compulsory school age: from when the parents choose to enrol the child, no later than **age 6**, until 18 (24 P.S. 13-1326). Effective 2020-21 (Act 16 of 2019): a child who is 6 on or before September 1 must enrol or begin a home education program that year. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/policies-acts-and-laws/basic-education-circulars-becs/purdons-statutes/compulsory-school-attendance-unlawful-absences-and-school-attendance-improvement-conferences ; 22 Pa. Code 11.13 https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter11/022_0011.pdf
- **Kindergarten is not compulsory**: "Kindergarten is not compulsory. Compulsory attendance begins at age six." Programs are "established and maintained by local school boards for children between the ages of four and six"; a half-day program needs at least 2.5 hours and a full day at least 5 instructional hours; the board sets the entry age, not under 4 years 0 months before the first day of the term. https://www.pa.gov/agencies/education/resources/policies-acts-and-laws/basic-education-circulars-becs/purdons-statutes/admission-to-kindergarten-and-beginners (BEC reviewed July/August 2026). Section 11.14 is titled "Admission to kindergarten **when provided**."
- Whether the School Code *requires* every district to offer kindergarten: **UNCONFIRMED** (regulations and PDE speak of kindergarten "when provided"/"if offered", and a Commonwealth Court note in 22 Pa. Code 11.14 says a child "is not statutorily entitled to a kindergarten education"). Safest guide wording: kindergarten is optional by law, and most districts offer it.
- The primary program (K to grade 3) is "ordinarily completed by children who are approximately 6" and the intermediate level by about age 11; planned instruction every year includes ELA, math, science/environment/ecology, technology and engineering, social studies, health/safety/PE, the arts, career education and personal finance (22 Pa. Code 4.21). Before the end of elementary school every student must get planned instruction at least once in **history of the United States, history of the Commonwealth, geography and civics** (4.21(g)). https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.21.html

### Standards: who adopts what, and when (22 Pa. Code 4.12; State Board list)
Sources: https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/022/chapter4/s4.12.html and https://www.pa.gov/agencies/stateboard/resources/regulations--policy/state-academic-standards . The State Board says the standards "are promulgated as state regulations. As such, they must be used as the basis for curriculum and instruction in Pennsylvania's public schools."
- **English language arts and math**: Pennsylvania Core Standards, Appendix A-2, effective **March 1, 2014** (4.12(a)(8), (9)). No revision found.
- **Science**: **Pennsylvania Integrated Standards for Science, Environment, Ecology, Technology and Engineering (Grades K-5)**; **Pennsylvania Integrated Standards for Science, Environment and Ecology (Grades 6-12)**; **Pennsylvania Technology and Engineering Standards (Grades 6-12)**. Published in the Pennsylvania Bulletin **July 16, 2022**, **effective July 1, 2025** (Appendix B-1; the older Appendix B expired that day). Written in three dimensions (practices, core ideas, crosscutting concepts) and based on the NRC framework and NGSS with environmental literacy and technology and engineering added. PSSA science for grades 5 and 8 tests them from 2025-26. "STEELS" is PDE's working name; **a 2023 revision is UNCONFIRMED** (the Board's page and the PDFs show only the 2022 publication and 2025 effective date). K-5 PDF https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/stateboard/documents/regulations-and-statements/state-academic-standards/pa%20integrated%20standards%20for%20science%20environment%20ecology%20technology%20and%20engineering-%20k-5.pdf ; 6-12 PDF https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/stateboard/documents/regulations-and-statements/state-academic-standards/pennsylvania%20integrated%20standards%20for%20science%20environment%20and%20ecology%20-%20grades%206-12.pdf
- **Social studies**:
  - **History, Geography, Civics and Government**: the **2003** standards (Appendix C, adopted January 10, 2003, effective January 12, 2003, 33 Pa.B.; the State Board page gives the Pennsylvania Bulletin publication date as January 11, 2003). **Not revised**; I found no 2026 proposal to revise them. The Board must review standards between every 5 and 10 years (4.12(i)).
  - **Economics**: new standards, Appendix C-1, published Feb. 8, 2025, **effective July 1, 2026** (the 2003 economics standards expired that day).
  - **Personal Finance** (new standalone standards, Appendix F) and **Family and Consumer Sciences** (D-1): effective July 1, 2026. 4.21 requires personal finance instruction in elementary years.
  - So the answer to "civics 2026 revision": **no**; the 2026 revisions are economics, personal finance, career education and work, and family and consumer sciences.
- **Career Education and Work**: Appendix E-1, effective **July 1, 2026** (see section 4).
- **Arts and humanities** and **Health, Safety and Physical Education**: 2003.
- Pennsylvania Core Standards for **Reading and Writing in History and Social Studies / Science and Technical Subjects** are appendices to the history and science standards (2014).
- **State standards, local curriculum ("local control")**: "It is the policy of the Board that the local curriculum be designed by school entities to achieve the academic standards"; "The Department may not, and the Board will not, require school entities to utilize a Statewide curriculum or Statewide reading lists." (22 Pa. Code 4.4(a), (f)). The Department offers a voluntary model curriculum (4.4(e)). So units, order and textbooks differ by district; a guide should say "your district's curriculum may order topics differently".

## 6. Native nations and Pennsylvania history (for light, present-tense content)

- The Commonwealth's own children's page: "Pennsylvania was first and originally inhabited by the indigenous people from the **Susquehannock, Lenape (or Delaware), Shawnee, and Nanticoke** tribes"; Harrisburg sits where the Susquehannocks and Lenape lived, along with the Shawnee and Nanticoke. https://www.pa.gov/governor/kids-corner
- State Library of Pennsylvania: contact-era tribes included the Lenni Lenape (Delaware) and Susquehannock, who made treaties with William Penn; the Walking Purchase of 1737 alienated Native people; the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois or Six Nations) "claimed suzerainty over Pennsylvania's tribes" and allowed displaced groups such as the Conoy and Nanticoke to settle. It also says the colonial period left "no recognized surviving Native tribes in Pennsylvania" and that it was "slaughter and displacement" (a library essay; avoid its phrasing for living peoples). https://digitalcollections.statelibrary.pa.gov/america250pa-and-the-state-library-of-pennsylvania/america250-special-populations
- **No federally recognized tribe resides in Pennsylvania, and the state has no tribal lands**: "Although no Federally-recognized tribes currently reside within the Commonwealth, their ties to this region go back thousands of years" and "Pennsylvania does not currently contain any tribal lands." PennDOT's Tribal Consultation Handbook (Pub 591) https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/penndot/documents/public/pubsforms/publications/pub%20591.pdf . That handbook says 14 federally recognized tribes have ancestral ties; PennDOT's 2020 overview says **15** (https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/penndot/documents/programs-and-doing-business/roadwaydesignenvironment/environment/culturalresources/tribal%20governance-history_consultation%20document.pdf ) and its current web page says **16** (https://www.pa.gov/agencies/penndot/programs-and-doing-business/environment/cultural-resources-management-program/archaeology-tribes ). Do not print a number; say "more than a dozen".
- The nations PennDOT and the Federal Highway Administration consult, by group (2020 overview; names as in the Bureau of Indian Affairs list of Jan. 30, 2026, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-30/html/2026-01899.htm ; each seat is the address on the PennDOT document):
  - **Delaware (Lenape)**: **Delaware Nation, Oklahoma** (Anadarko, Oklahoma); **Delaware Tribe of Indians** (Bartlesville, Oklahoma); **Stockbridge Munsee Community, Wisconsin** (Bowler, Wisconsin). **CORRECTION:** the official BIA name is "Stockbridge Munsee Community, Wisconsin" (the PennDOT document says "Stockbridge Munsee Band of Mohican Indians of Wisconsin" and "Stockbridge-Munsee Community" in its heading); the brief's "Stockbridge-Munsee Community" is acceptable.
  - **Shawnee**: Absentee-Shawnee Tribe of Indians of Oklahoma (Shawnee, OK); Eastern Shawnee Tribe of Oklahoma (Wyandotte, OK); Shawnee Tribe (Miami, OK).
  - **Haudenosaunee (Six Nations)**: Seneca Nation of Indians (Salamanca, New York); Tonawanda Band of Seneca (Basom, New York); Seneca-Cayuga Nation (Grove, OK); Cayuga Nation (Seneca Falls, NY); Oneida Indian Nation (Oneida, NY); Oneida Nation of Wisconsin; Onondaga Nation (Nedrow, NY); Tuscarora Nation (Lewiston, NY); St. Regis Mohawk Tribe (Akwesasne, NY).
- **Canadian descendant communities**: PHMC's annual report lists five "nations of the Delaware diaspora" who reburied about 180 Lenape ancestors at Pennsbury Manor starting April 11, 2022: Delaware Nation (Oklahoma), Delaware Tribe of Indians (Oklahoma), Stockbridge-Munsee (Wisconsin), **Munsee-Delaware Nation (Ontario)** and **Delaware Nation of Moraviantown (Ontario)** (PHMC's name; the community is also known as Moravian of the Thames: **UNCONFIRMED** on any Pennsylvania site). The report calls all five "federally recognized Delaware tribes"; the two Ontario nations are First Nations in Canada, so do not call them federally recognized in the US. https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/phmc/documents/about/public-information/documents/phmc_annual_report_2021_2022.pdf
- **Seneca and Cornplanter**: in 1791 the Seneca leader Cornplanter received three land grants from Pennsylvania on the Allegheny River just south of the New York border; his community on the remaining one was Cornplanter's Town. "Although this land was never officially recognized by the state of Pennsylvania as an Indian reservation," it served that purpose for more than 150 years, "the only self-governing Indian community within the state." The Kinzua Dam (construction began 1959) flooded land; about 550 Seneca lost homes, including those on the Cornplanter Tract, and most resettled on other Seneca lands in western New York. https://explorepahistory.com/hmarker.php?markerId=1-A-22D (Pennsylvania Historical Marker program text)
- **Lenape and William Penn**: in 1682 Penn and Tamanend, chief of the Lenape (Delaware) nation in the Delaware Valley, agreed to a perpetual peace; PHMC states Penn's sons broke the agreement (Walking Purchase). PHMC report above.
- **Susquehannock**: the Conestoga remnant lived in Lancaster County on Conestoga Manor, a tract provided under a treaty with Penn. Source: search summary of a Commonwealth page; the page itself was not read, so **UNCONFIRMED** for exact wording; PHMC press also lists Barry Kent's *Susquehanna's Indians* as the reference. Do not state numbers or the end of the Susquehannock without reading it.
- **Erie (Eriez)**: Pennsylvania's Erie lake region name; the Erie as a nation is **UNCONFIRMED** here (only a search-result summary of a State Museum page); skip it unless a writer reads the source.
- **State recognition**: a Pennsylvania process or list of state-recognized tribes: **UNCONFIRMED**; none found on pa.gov. Say only "federally recognized Nations with ancestral ties, based in other states".
- Present-tense, living-community project: the Shapiro Administration's Pennsylvania Tourism Office, with Lehigh University's Institute for Indigenous Studies and federally recognized nations "originally from Pennsylvania", is developing Indigenous heritage experiences (announced Oct. 13, 2023). https://dced.pa.gov/newsroom/shapiro-administration-announces-indigenous-peoples-cultural-and-heritage-initiative/

## 7. Pennsylvania facts a content writer could get wrong

Verified on the Governor's "PA Kids' Corner" (https://www.pa.gov/governor/kids-corner) unless another URL is given; the Pennsylvania Constitution text was read at https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/CT/HTM/00/00.htm .
- **It is a Commonwealth**, "technically a commonwealth and not a state", with no difference in how it is governed. Write "the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania" in formal places.
- **Nickname: the Keystone State** (the "Keystone State" title stuck in the 1800s). **Capital: Harrisburg**, on the Susquehanna River, in south-central Pennsylvania. **State motto: "Virtue, Liberty and Independence"** (the Kids' Corner prints it without commas).
- **Ratification**: Pennsylvania ratified the U.S. Constitution on **December 12, 1787**, the second state (Delaware was first, Dec. 7, 1787). Pennsylvania's own statute: "Pennsylvania, which on December 12, 1787, became the second state to ratify the United States Constitution." Act of June 3, 1987, No. 7: https://www.palegis.us/statutes/unconsolidated/law-information/view-statute?txtType=PDF&SessYr=1987&ActNum=0007.&SessInd=0 ; Census state guide https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/pennsylvania.html . (The State Library gives December 13 for the signed form; use December 12.)
- **67 counties** (Census: "There are 67 counties in Pennsylvania"; Philadelphia city and county are consolidated; DCED uses the same count: https://dced.pa.gov/download/local-government-fact-sheet/?wpdmdl=69447&ind=0 ).
- **Legislature: the General Assembly**, a Senate and a House of Representatives (Constitution Art. II section 1). The Commonwealth is divided into **50 senatorial and 203 representative districts**, each electing one member (Art. II section 16). Senators serve four years, Representatives two (Art. II section 3). **Governor: four-year term, eligible to succeed himself for one additional term** (Art. IV section 3). The Governor's term starts the third Tuesday of January after election. Write "two consecutive terms", not "two terms total" (a former governor can run again later: **UNCONFIRMED**, don't state it).
- **Constitution**: the current Constitution is called the **Constitution of 1968**; the 1874 Constitution was modified and renumbered by amendments in 1966, 1967 and April 23, 1968 (1 Pa.C.S. 906). The Constitution of 1874 itself was ratified December 16, 1873. Same Constitution page.
- **State symbols** (Kids' Corner): flower **mountain laurel** (designated May 5, 1933); tree **eastern hemlock** (1931); animal **white-tailed deer** (October 2, 1959); bird **ruffed grouse** (June 22, 1931); dog **Great Dane** (1965); fish **brook trout** (March 9, 1970); insect **firefly** (April 10, 1974); fossil **Eldredgeops rana, a trilobite** (December 5, 1988); beverage **milk** (April 29, 1982); amphibian **eastern hellbender** (2019). **CORRECTION:** the state tree is the *eastern* hemlock; the firefly is a beetle, not a fly.
- Size and population (Kids' Corner, rounded): about 309 miles long and 174 miles wide, over 46,000 square miles, 33rd in area, nearly 13 million people. Harrisburg has "a little over 50,000" people and is the ninth largest city.
- **Name**: "Pennsylvania" means "Penn's Woods" in Latin; the English king gave the colony to William Penn, who arrived in 1681 (Kids' Corner). **Coat of arms** (on the flag, based on a 1778 design by Caleb Lownes): a shield with a ship, a clay pillow (as printed; **UNCONFIRMED** meaning, probably a plow) and three sheaves of wheat, held by two horses with a bald eagle above, an olive branch and a cornstalk below, and the motto on the bottom. Check the official blazon before writing about the arms.
