Every benchmark is cited by at least one unit, but some are only touched lightly (a few questions in a broader unit), and a few
need a map or free text the question types can't give:
- K.A.1.1, K.A.1.2, 1.A.1.2, 2.A.1.2, 3.A.1.2: using a timeline, a primary source and the media center or technology are practised
  through adopted general units (time words, "clues from the past", "ask and find"), not Florida-specific ones.
- 3.G.2.3: labelling every state in each of the five regions needs a map; fl-g3-us-regions asks about a dozen well-known states.
- 3.G.2.6: how people see places differently (interviews, mental maps, poems and legends) has one question; it needs open work.
- 4.AA.1.1: the standard's examples are cut off in the data file after Zora Neale Hurston, the Florida Highwaymen and Mary McLeod
  Bethune, so the unit names those three. Check the book for the remaining examples.
- 4.FL.*: all 28 financial-literacy benchmarks are cited by two units (fl-g4-earning-income, fl-g4-spending-saving-risk) plus
  Oregon's "Earning & Saving"; each benchmark gets a handful of questions, not a unit of its own.
- 3.G.2.1 to 3.G.4.3, 3.E.1.4: the countries, landmarks, climate and money of Canada, Mexico and the Caribbean are covered by
  Florida's own units (fl-g3-north-america, fl-g3-people-and-money), which is why they may name Canada. Written from general
  knowledge: the owner should read the Caribbean and Mexico facts before launch.
- Seminole and Miccosukee content (fl-g4-first-floridians, fl-g4-seminole-wars) is light, factual and in the present tense for
  today's nations, and should have partner review before it grows.
