          C3 Teaching and Learning K-8 Framework –
                Sample Scope and Sequence
Kindergarten – Me and My Community
The focus of kindergarten is to provide students with opportunities to explore topics that
affect them and their personal environments. These include basic citizenship skills such
as rule making and compliance, and how these change in different settings (school,
home, community). Students will engage in learning about themselves, their schools,
their cities/towns, and local communities. They will also develop an awareness of the
similarities and differences among individuals in the classroom as well as within the
school and community. Students will have the opportunity to compare how life in the
past is different from today, with respect to their own experiences within their home,
classroom, and community.

Grade 1 – People, Places, and the Environment
In grade 1, students will learn how they are connected to the world around them and
develop a deeper understanding of how diverse cultures, values, and beliefs help to
shape the world in which we live. Students will further develop a sense of purpose and
empathy regarding their role and the role of other citizens within the community and
world. As a global citizen, it is developmentally appropriate for students to identify
similarities and differences from others (cultural, likes and dislikes, gender, skin color,
hair, and eye color). Best practice is having materials and visuals that are
representative of people, cultures, and the community in which they live. They will have
the opportunity to look for ways that they can be civic-minded and make change happen
within their own classroom, school, and community. Students will develop an
understanding of how basic economic concepts influence people, places, and the
environment. They will learn that people not only use the environment to meet their
needs and wants but also that the environment often dictates where and how people
choose to live. Students will continue building on group skills, including roles, rules,
voicing an opinion, and conflict resolution skills with adult support. They will enhance
their knowledge of spatial awareness of their surroundings by furthering the use of
geographic representations, tools, and terminology to process information about the
world around them. Students will continue to compare life in the past to life today as
they study the contributions of historical figures and how those figures have helped
shape and change various communities and environments across the
globe.

2nd Grade – The Americas
In grade 2, students will expand on their understanding of the concept of community by
exploring past and present neighboring communities in Central America, North America,
South America, and the Caribbean. Students will explain continuities and differences
among cultures, governments, traditions, and available resources to investigate how
individuals work together while acknowledging different perspectives and cultivating
empathy. Students will learn how culture spreads across borders and compare and
contrast Vermont/local community governments, customs, and traditions to others in the
Americas. Students will explain current and historic patterns of human settlement and
compare the ways various cultural groups connect and interact across the Americas.
Students will describe how resources vary across geography and climates and discuss
how resources can be categorized as renewable and non-renewable.

3rd Grade – Global Interactions
In grade 3, students will expand on their understanding of the concept of community
and widen their global lens by exploring interactions between groups of people in Africa,
Asia, Europe, and Oceania. Students will explain continuities and differences among
cultures, governments, traditions, and resources to investigate how individuals work
together while acknowledging different perspectives and cultivating empathy. Students
will learn how culture spreads across borders and compare and contrast Vermont/local
community customs and traditions to others in the world. Students will explain current
and historic patterns of human settlement and compare the ways various cultural
groups connect and interact. Students will describe how resources vary across
geography and climates and discuss how resources can be categorized as renewable
and non-renewable.

4th Grade – Vermont, the Early Republic, and the
United States.: Pre-Colonial Times to the Civil War
In grade 4, students will examine change and continuity in Vermont and United States
history from pre-colonial times to the Civil War. Students will understand the concept of
movement and its impact on people, goods, and ideas; learn how natural resources
have influenced economic development; and examine the conflicts and compromises
that resulted from migration and settlement. This learning will allow students to
understand the tensions and factors that led to the fight for independence, the
establishment of the United States, and subsequent Vermont statehood. Students will
prepare for their role as responsible and informed citizens by examining the Vermont
Constitution and the U.S. Constitution, including the determinants of power and its
effects; exploring the development, structure, and function of the United States

5th Grade – World Geography and Ancient Civilizations
I: Western Asia, the Middle East, North Africa, Sub-
Saharan Africa
In grade 5, students will explore how people moved, traded, and interacted with others
in early civilizations. They will learn how geography, power, wealth, and religion shaped
the growth and culture of these societies. Students will focus on regions like Western
Asia, the Middle East, North Africa, and Sub-Saharan Africa. They will also learn about
important cultural contributions and lasting achievements. By studying ancient history,
students will better understand the roots of the modern world.

6th Grade – World Geography and Ancient Civilization
II: South and East Asia, Oceania, Latin America, and
Europe
In grade 6, students will examine how movement and migration impacted the
interactions between expanding civilizations through conquest and trade, including the
physical and political geography and ancient societies of South and East Asia, Oceania,
Latin America, and Europe. Students will note how geography, power, wealth and
religion influence culture, change, and development within civilizations as they examine
how the perspectives of political science, economics, geography, history, and
archaeology apply to the study of regions and countries. Students will develop an
appreciation of cultural contributions (art, music, literature, architecture) and lasting
achievements in science and technology. Grade 6 concludes with a study of
government in Greece and Rome, which serves as a prelude to the study of civics in
grade 8. Through an understanding of ancient history, students will develop an
appreciation for the foundations of the modern world.

that continued into Reconstruction and beyond and led to the long struggle in the 19th
and 20th centuries for civil rights. Grade 7 content spans decades; takes students

8th Grade – Civics and Economics in Action
In grade 8, students will utilize past knowledge and understanding to expand their focus
on the core concepts and principles underlying America’s democracy; the structure and
limits of the national government as set forth in the United States Constitution, including
an examination of democratic ideals; and the role of the citizen in a democratic society.
Students will collaborate to identify a problem(s); develop solutions that would require
government involvement; and participate in student-led civics projects focused on
developing civic knowledge, skills, dispositions, and media literacy. Students will also
learn about the workings of both national and global economies and develop an
understanding of the decision-making processes of individuals, businesses, and
governments and how they are connected. Gaining an understanding of economic
resources, such as human capital, land, technology, and investments, and how they
play a role in big-picture decision making in a global environment will assist students in
making informed decisions in a wide variety of contexts.
Note: This class is not seen as a replacement for higher-level analysis of civic and
personal finance concepts at the high school level, but rather as a foundation for deeper
learning in financial literacy and civics/American Government.
