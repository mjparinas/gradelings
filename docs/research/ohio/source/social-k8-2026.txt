Strand: History
TOPIC: OHIO – PAST, PRESENT, AND FUTURE
6. American Indians shaped the territory that is present day Ohio through trade, farming, hunting, and fishing. The arrival of
    immigrants and migrating settlers altered American Indian culture and trade interactions, resulting in lasting impacts.
7. American Indians experienced ongoing conflicts as migrating settlers and immigrants moved into the Ohio Country. Ongoing
    frontier conflicts contributed to the exclusion and removal of American Indians.
8. The Declaration of Independence outlined the colonial objections to British rule and led to the American Revolution. American
    independence led to expanded western settlement and diplomatic relations with Europe.
9. Ohio became a state in 1803 following the procedures established in the Northwest Ordinance. Upon achieving statehood, Ohio
    and its citizens were guaranteed rights and protections under the U.S. Constitution.
10. Ohioans have made significant contributions to the United States since statehood, with their impact continuing today.

Strand: History
TOPIC: EARLY CIVILIZATIONS
5. The Greek and Roman civilizations significantly influenced later societies through their contributions to governance, law,
   engineering, technology, art, architecture, literature, and history. The Roman Empire also facilitated the spread of Christianity.

TOPIC: FEUDALISM AND TRANSITIONS
6. The Roman Empire collapsed due to various internal and external factors (political, social and economic) which led to the
   development of feudalism and the manorial system in the region. The fall of Rome and later invasions also allowed for the creation
   of new empires in the region.
7. The Mongols conquered much of Asia which led to unified states in China and Korea. Mongol failure to conquer Japan allowed a
   feudal system to persist.

Strand: History
TOPIC: JOURNEY TO INDEPENDENCE
6. Europeans encountered American Indian civilizations as they explored and colonized North America for economic and religious

7. Europeans, colonists, and American Indians cooperated, negotiated, and clashed while competing for land and resources in North
   America.
8. Race-based slavery transformed the political, economic, and social systems of the British colonies and continued to impact the
   United States in the 18th and 19th centuries.
9. The Declaration of Independence established the purpose of the American Revolution, highlighting the grievances against the
   British Empire and proclaiming the natural rights of citizens and the powers of the new nation.

TOPIC: FORMING A MORE PERFECT UNION
10. The Articles of Confederation represented the first attempt of creating a central government that addressed the new political,
    economic, and territorial landscapes the United States faced. The Northwest Ordinance of 1787 established the process for
    statehood.
11. The U.S. Constitution established a federal government with increased powers. The Federalist and Antifederalist Papers’
    contrasting arguments led to the development of the Bill of Rights, protecting individual liberties and limiting government power.
12. The United States balanced domestic and foreign challenges in the late 1700s and early 1800s by testing the limits of constitutional
    federal power, promoting domestic stability, acquiring new lands, and moderating international tensions.
13. The United States expanded the frontier westward by adding territory through armed conflict, treaties, and land purchases.
    Westward expansion and the concept of Manifest Destiny increased the United States’ boundaries and population while forcibly
    removing American Indian nations from native lands.

TOPIC: PRESERVING THE UNION
14. Sectional divisions over political, economic, and social issues surrounding slavery in the 1800s led to the American Civil War.
15. The American Civil War transformed the United States politically, socially, and economically as the key events and significant
    figures influenced the Civil War’s course and outcomes.
16. The Reconstruction period attempted to redefine social and political structures through changes to the U.S. Constitution and
    expanded federal authority. Lingering diﬀerences created ongoing tensions between federal power and state interests in the South.

Strand: Geography
TOPIC: HUMAN SYSTEMS
17. Push and pull factors impacted the expansion of the United States environmentally, economically, politically, and socially.
18. Cultural biases, stereotypes and prejudices had social, political and economic impacts on diﬀerent groups in the United States.

Strand: Government
TOPIC: ROLES AND SYSTEMS OF GOVERNMENT
19. The U.S. Constitution created a federal democratic republic with elected representatives, separation of powers, and checks and
    balances.
20. The U.S. Constitution protects citizens’ rights by limiting the powers of government.
