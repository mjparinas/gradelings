Introduction
Overview
Ohio’s Learning Standards for English Language Arts (2026) reflect the state’s ongoing commitment to ensuring that every student builds strong
foundations in language and literacy. These revised standards were developed through a collaborative process that engaged educators,
specialists, and stakeholders from across Ohio.

The standards in this document provide clear expectations for what students should know and be able to do at the end of each grade level. They
emphasize essential skills in reading, writing, speaking, listening, and language, while also supporting coherence and alignment across the
kindergarten – 12th grade continuum.

These revisions reaﬀirm Ohio’s dedication to ensuring all students have access to rigorous, meaningful, and well-supported literacy learning
aligned with the Science of Reading. The Ohio Department of Education and Workforce extends its gratitude to the educators who contributed
their expertise throughout this process and to the schools and districts who continue to champion strong literacy outcomes for every child.

Ohio’s schools and districts must use core curriculum and instructional materials in English language arts only from the List of High-Quality Core
Curriculum and Instructional Materials in English Language Arts (prekindergarten-grade 5), which may follow diﬀerent scope and sequences for
reading and writing foundational skills. It is important to note that there is no one perfect scope and sequence; however, skills should build from
basic to advanced as students progress through grades. For the purpose of providing a robust scope and sequence of decoding and encoding
skills, the Department utilized the LETRS (Language Essentials for Teachers of Reading and Spelling) progression as an example. For
implementation purposes, please follow the scope and sequence of the school or district’s adopted core curriculum and English language arts
instructional materials.

English Language Arts Strands
Reading Literature Standards outline what students should know and be able to do as they engage with stories, drama, and poetry across
grade levels. The standards progress from foundational comprehension in the early grades to deep literary analysis in high school.

Reading Informational Standards outline expectations for how students engage with nonfiction texts, including historical, scientific, and
technical sources. Across the K–12 continuum, students move from basic listening and comprehension skills to deep analysis, evaluation of
arguments, and integration of information across multiple sources and media.

Reading Foundations Standards serve as the fundamental components of skilled reading. These standards emphasize print concepts,
phonological awareness, phonics and decoding, as well as fluency. In higher grades, students also engage with advanced word analysis,
morphology, and fluency, which equip them with foundational skills necessary for understanding complex literary and informational texts in the
future. This strand is available in grades kindergarten-grade 8.

Writing Foundations Standards provide systematic instruction in the early and intermediate skills students need to write fluently, legibly, and
accurately. They emphasize handwriting, encoding/spelling, and sentence-level conventions, supporting strong writing across genres and
content areas. This strand is available in grades kindergarten-grade 8.

Writing Standards describe what students must know and be able to do to communicate eﬀectively through written language. Across grade
levels, students learn to produce increasingly sophisticated writing for a variety of tasks, purposes, and audiences. The standards address three
core genres (opinion, informative, and narrative), research and inquiry, technology use, and writing processes such as planning, revising, editing,
and rewriting.

Speaking and Listening Standards outline how students develop skills to participate eﬀectively in discussions, interpret and analyze
information presented in various formats, present ideas clearly, and adapt language to context. The standards progress from foundational
conversation skills in the early grades to advanced communication, analysis, and presentation skills in high school.

Language Standards describe what students should know and be able to do related to grammar, usage, mechanics, vocabulary, and language
conventions. These expectations build steadily from foundational grammar and word learning strategies in early grades to advanced command
of English conventions and nuanced language use in high school.

Standards for Literacy in History/Social Studies, Science, and Technical Subjects
In 2017, the Department adopted Standards for Literacy in History/Social Studies, Science and Technical Subjects for grades 6-12. High-quality
instruction grounded in the Science of Reading is necessary for all students, including those in grades 6-12. Adolescent literacy focuses on these
students, providing opportunities to continue evidence-based practices in literacy skills, such as advanced word study, vocabulary, fluency,
comprehension, and writing. As students progress through secondary education, they will continue to apply these skills to complex texts, as well
as transferring these skills across content areas. Without high-quality adolescent literacy instruction, students may struggle with using content-
area vocabulary to analyze texts, evaluate claims across multiple texts, follow complex multistep procedures in an experiment, or formally
communicate thoughts and ideas through research and essays.

The standards below begin at grade 6; standards for K–5 reading in history/social studies, science, and technical subjects are integrated into the
K–5 Reading standards. It is important to note that the grade 6–12 literacy standards in history/social studies, science, and technical subjects do
not replace content standards in those areas but rather supplement them. Schools may incorporate these standards into their standards for
those subjects or adopt them as content area literacy standards.
