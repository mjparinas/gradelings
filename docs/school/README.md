# Using Gradelings in a school or district

Everything here is a **draft prepared for the owner**. None of it has been reviewed by a lawyer, and the contract and consent texts must be before anyone signs or sends them. Placeholders are marked `[...]`.

BC has no provincial approval list: each of the 60 districts decides for itself, usually through a Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) under FIPPA, and sometimes a Supplemental Review when data leaves Canada. See `/mnt/project-files/school-approval/bc-school-approval-gaps.md` in the project files for the research behind this. Ontario boards run their own privacy reviews on the same pattern.

## What the product does today

- A teacher has an ordinary account (confirmed email) and creates classes for one province's curriculum.
- **Two ways to get students in.** A parent can link their own child with the class code (the parent decides, and can unlink). Or the teacher adds students by first name or nickname, and each gets a login code. There is no email or password for a student.
- A student account holds a first name or nickname, the class's grade, an avatar and practice results. It has no parent area, no billing and no way to see anything but its own play.
- The teacher sees first name, grade, avatar and results on the units they assigned, plus a "what to look at next" list.
- Removing a student, closing a class and deleting a teacher account each delete the students' data at once.

## Schools in the United States (Washington, Oregon, California, New York, Texas, Maine, Minnesota, Alaska, Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Illinois, Colorado, Pennsylvania, Georgia, Ohio, New Jersey, Indiana, Virginia, Missouri and North Carolina)

Classes can follow Washington, Oregon, California, New York, Texas, Maine, Minnesota, Alaska, Alabama, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Illinois, Colorado, Pennsylvania, Georgia, Ohio, New Jersey, Indiana, Virginia, Missouri or North Carolina (`TEACHER_FRAMEWORK_IDS` in `src/components/site/teachers.ts`).

- **Consent.** Under COPPA a school can consent on parents' behalf for educational use. Student accounts a teacher adds are never asked for parental consent (only family accounts are; `src/lib/coppa.ts`), and the teacher's screens for a US class carry one line saying the school consents on parents' behalf for educational use only (`US_CLASS_CONSENT` in `src/lib/classroom.ts`).
- **FERPA.** For a school, the Provider acts as a "school official" with a legitimate educational interest: it works under the school's direct control, uses education records only for the purpose they were shared for, and doesn't re-disclose them. Section 12 of `school-data-agreement.md` says so.
- **Data agreement.** Washington, Oregon, California, Texas and Alabama districts mostly sign the Student Data Privacy Consortium's National Data Privacy Agreement (NDPA) with their state's exhibit (in Washington through the Washington Student Privacy Alliance, in Oregon through the Oregon Student Privacy Alliance, in California through the California Student Privacy Alliance, in Texas through the Texas Student Privacy Alliance, in Alabama through the Alabama Student Privacy Alliance; ask a Maine, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, Florida, Illinois, Colorado, Pennsylvania or Missouri district which version it uses). Offer to sign that rather than our own template; `school-data-agreement.md` section 12 lists what we can commit to.
- **Washington law.** `/privacy/` ("Families in the United States") states our SUPER Act commitments (no targeted advertising, no sale, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, deletion on request, a security program, notice before material changes) and that we collect no consumer health data under the My Health My Data Act.
- **Oregon law.** `/privacy/` states the matching commitments under the Oregon Student Information Protection Act (ORS 336.184): no targeted advertising, no sale, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, sharing only as the law allows, a security program and deletion when the school asks.
- **California law.** `/privacy/` states the matching commitments under SOPIPA (Business and Professions Code 22584): no targeted advertising, no sale, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, sharing only as the law allows, a security program and deletion when the school asks. A California district's contract must carry the terms in Education Code 49073.1 (AB 1584); `school-data-agreement.md` and the NDPA's California exhibit cover them.
- **New York law.** `/privacy/` states our commitments under Education Law 2-d and Part 121 of the Commissioner's regulations: no sale, no use or release for marketing or commercial purposes, use only for the school's educational purposes, sharing only with the listed service providers, a security program, deletion when the school asks and telling the school about a breach affecting its students. Every New York district contract must carry the district's Parents' Bill of Rights for Data Privacy and Security and supplemental information about the product, and Part 121 asks a contractor for a data security and privacy plan, encryption in transit and at rest, security practices aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and breach notice to the district within 7 calendar days of discovery. Encryption at rest (Turso) is still a **(confirm)** item, and the breach window in `school-data-agreement.md` section 7 is blank; settle both before signing with a New York district.
- **Texas law.** `/privacy/` states the matching commitments under Texas Education Code chapter 32, subchapter D (sections 32.151 to 32.157): no targeted advertising, no sale or rental, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, sharing only as the law allows, a security program and deletion when the school asks.
- **Maine law.** `/privacy/` states the matching commitments under Maine's Student Information Privacy Act (20-A M.R.S. sections 951 to 953): no targeted advertising, no sale, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, sharing only as the law allows, a security program and deletion when the school asks.
- **Minnesota law.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as a technology provider under Minnesota Statutes 13.32, subdivisions 13 and 14 (added in 2022): student information belongs to the school, no sale, sharing or dissemination beyond what the law allows, no commercial use (marketing or advertising to students or parents included), a security program, breach information to the school so it can meet section 13.055, and deletion or return within 90 days after the contract ends unless renewal is expected. Subdivision 14 bars monitoring location, camera, microphone, keystrokes or browsing on a school-issued device; the app uses none of these. A technology provider is also bound by section 13.05, subdivision 11 (a government contractor's duties under the Data Practices Act).
- **Alaska.** Alaska has no student online privacy statute of its own. `/privacy/` gives Alaska schools the same commitments as other states (no targeted advertising, no sale, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, sharing only with the listed providers, a security program, deletion when the school asks) and says we tell the school about a breach affecting its students, in line with the Alaska Personal Information Protection Act (AS 45.48). FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Alabama law.** Alabama has no student privacy statute of its own beyond FERPA. The State Board of Education's Data Use Governance Policy has every district adopt a local student records governance and use policy and share data with service providers only under a signed agreement, so an Alabama district will ask for its own agreement or the NDPA. `/privacy/` states the same commitments as for the other states: no targeted advertising, no sale, no profiling beyond the school's purpose, sharing only as the law allows, a security program and deletion when the school asks. The Alabama Personal Data Protection Act (2026, in force May 1, 2027) leaves out data FERPA covers, and treats a known child's data under COPPA.
- **Arizona.** `/privacy/` states our Arizona Revised Statutes section 15-1046 (student data privacy) commitments: no targeted advertising, no selling or renting, no profile beyond the school's purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), a security program, and deletion within a reasonable time when the school asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Arkansas.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as an operator under Arkansas's Student Online Personal Information Protection Act (Arkansas Code 6-18-109): no targeted advertising, no sale, no profile beyond the school's purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), a security program, and deletion when the school asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Florida.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as an operator under Florida's Student Online Personal Information Protection Act (section 1006.1494, Florida Statutes): no targeted advertising, no sharing, selling or renting, no profile beyond the school's purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), collection limited to what the service needs, a security program, and deletion when the school asks, including within 90 days of the district telling us a student is no longer enrolled. State Board of Education Rule 6A-1.09550 sets what a district's student data policy and vendor contracts must include; offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA with Florida's exhibit if it asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Illinois.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as an operator under the Illinois Student Online Personal Protection Act (SOPPA, 105 ILCS 85, as amended in 2021): no targeted advertising, no sale or rental, no profile beyond the school's purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), reasonable security, breach notice to the school within 30 calendar days of finding it, and deletion when the school asks. SOPPA has an Illinois school share covered information with an operator only under a written agreement, which the school must publish with a list of its operators; offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA with Illinois's exhibit if it asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Colorado.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as a provider under Colorado's Student Data Transparency and Security Act (C.R.S. 22-16-101 to 22-16-112): we say what student information we collect and why, use it only for the school's educational purposes, never sell it, never use it for targeted advertising, never build a profile beyond those purposes, bind our listed providers to the same limits by contract, keep a security program in place, tell the school about a breach, and delete the information when the school asks or the agreement ends. Colorado districts publish the providers they use and may sign a contract that adds terms; offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA with Colorado's exhibit if it asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Pennsylvania.** Pennsylvania has no student online privacy statute binding technology providers (Senate Bill 378 was laid on the table on June 3, 2026), so `/privacy/` gives Pennsylvania schools the same commitments as the other states', under the school's agreement and FERPA and COPPA. Pennsylvania's breach notification laws apply: under Act 151 of 2022 a vendor that holds a public school's data must tell the school "following discovery" of a breach (the statute sets no number of days; the seven-business-day deadline binds the school, not a vendor), and Act 33 of 2024 adds notice to the Attorney General when more than 500 residents are affected. Many districts sign the Student Data Privacy Consortium's agreement through the Pennsylvania Student Privacy Alliance; offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA if it asks. See `docs/research/pennsylvania/FACTS.md`.
- **Georgia.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as an operator under Georgia's Student Data Privacy, Accessibility, and Transparency Act (O.C.G.A. sections 20-2-660 to 20-2-668, 2015): no targeted advertising, no sale or rental, no profile beyond the school's purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), reasonable security, breach notice to the school without unreasonable delay (Georgia's breach law, O.C.G.A. 10-1-912, asks for the most expedient time possible), and deletion when the school asks. The Act also has the Georgia Department of Education and each district keep a data inventory and publish their privacy policies; offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA with Georgia's exhibit if it asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Ohio.** `/privacy/` states our commitments under Ohio Revised Code sections 3319.325 to 3319.327 (Senate Bill 29, in force October 2024): student records we hold for a school belong to the school; we protect them as Chapter 1347 asks of a school district; we never sell or share them, never use them for marketing or advertising and never use them beyond the school's educational purposes; only staff who need them for their work can see them; we never turn on location tracking, a microphone or a camera, or monitor a student's activity outside the app; we give the school what it needs to list us in its yearly notice to families; we tell the school if a breach affects its students; and we delete or return the records within 90 days after our agreement with the school ends, or sooner when the school asks.
- **New Jersey.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as an operator under New Jersey's law on student online education services (P.L.2019, c.494, N.J.S.A. 56:8-215 to 56:8-221, approved January 21, 2020): no targeted advertising, no sale or rental, no profile except for Kindergarten to Grade 12 school purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), reasonable security, breach notice to the school without unreasonable delay (New Jersey's breach law, N.J.S.A. 56:8-163, asks for the most expedient time possible), and deletion when the school or district asks, or when a student who has reached the age of majority asks. A violation is an unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud Act (56:8-220). FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Indiana.** Indiana has no student online privacy statute binding technology providers, so `/privacy/` gives Indiana schools the same commitments as the other states', under the school's agreement and FERPA and COPPA. Indiana's breach notification law (IC 24-4.9) has a business that keeps data for another tell its owner of a breach, and the owner notify residents without unreasonable delay and within 45 days; we promise the school notice without unreasonable delay and within 45 days at most. Offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA if it asks.
- **Virginia.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as a school service provider under the Code of Virginia, section 22.1-289.01: we use student personal information only for the school's educational purposes, never for targeted advertising, never sell it, never build a profile beyond those purposes, keep a security program in place, delete it when the school asks (within a reasonable time), say clearly what we collect and how we use it, and tell the school about a breach. Section 22.1-287.02 has the Department of Education and each school division keep their own data security policies, and many divisions sign the Virginia Student Data Privacy Consortium's agreement (the NDPA with Virginia's exhibit); offer the division's own agreement or that one if it asks. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.
- **Missouri.** Missouri has no student online privacy statute binding technology providers (section 161.096, RSMo, governs the state's own student data system and what DESE may collect), so `/privacy/` gives Missouri schools the same commitments as the other states', under the school's agreement and FERPA and COPPA. Missouri's breach notification law (section 407.1500, RSMo) asks anyone who maintains personal information for another to notify the owner immediately after discovering a breach; we tell the school without delay. Offer the district's own agreement or the NDPA if it asks (many Missouri districts sign it through the Missouri Student Privacy Alliance). See `docs/research/missouri/README.md`.
- **North Carolina.** `/privacy/` states our commitments as an operator under North Carolina's law on the protection of student data (G.S. 115C-401.2, 2015): no targeted advertising, no sale or rental, no profile beyond the school's K-12 purposes, disclosure only as the law allows (the school and the listed providers, who are contractually bound not to use or disclose it further and to keep it secure), reasonable security, breach notice to the school without unreasonable delay (North Carolina's Identity Theft Protection Act, G.S. 75-65, asks for notice without unreasonable delay), and deletion within 45 days when the school or district asks or tells us it has stopped using the service. Deleting a student in the teacher screens removes the data at once, which keeps the 45 days true. The statute is in `docs/research/north-carolina/source/law/`. FERPA and COPPA apply as everywhere.

## Still to do before a district can say yes

1. **Real legal entity and contact details** in `src/lib/brand.ts` (`LEGAL_NAME`, `CONTACT_EMAIL`, `MAILING_ADDRESS`), then a lawyer's review of the privacy policy, terms and the agreement.
2. **Provider answers** for the **(confirm)** items in the PIA pack (Turso encryption at rest and backups, database region, Resend and Sentry data handling).
3. **Screen reader testing** (VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA) and a keyboard run-through by a person, then update the conformance report.
4. **Decisions for the owner**, listed at the end of the PIA pack: whether a school plan exists (needs the owner's pricing decision) and whether co-teachers should be able to delete students or a school office role is needed. Co-teaching itself is built.
5. **A pilot** with one friendly school or district, which will show their actual process.
